📋 Background & Problem Statement

Kenya's maternal mortality ratio stands at 355 per 100,000 live births — five times the SDG 3.1 target — with the majority of preventable deaths concentrated among women who were never screened for risk factors during antenatal care.

Existing tools either operate at the population or county level, or are confined to hospital systems. No individual-level, Bayesian-augmented ML tool exists for use by Community Health Workers (CHWs) in Kenya using nationally representative KDHS data.

Research Gap: CHWs lack a data-driven, probabilistic triage tool calibrated to Kenyan demographic and geographic risk factors.

Dataset: Kenya Demographic & Health Survey 2022 Individual Recode (KDHS 2022-IR). After filtering for women with a recent birth: 10,391 records × 13 variables. Pilot testing uses 1,000 simulated records mirroring these distributions.

🎯 Research Objectives
  1. SO1 — ML Classification: Develop a TabNet / TabTransformer classification model to stratify pregnant women into Low, Moderate, and High risk categories using KDHS 2022 variables.
  2. SO2 — Bayesian Inference: Apply PyMC-based Bayesian inference to quantify prediction uncertainty and produce 95% highest-density credible intervals per patient.
  3. SO3 — AI-Integrated App: Deploy a bilingual (English / Kiswahili) Streamlit application with AI-generated clinical explanations for CHW use.

✅ Key Pilot Findings
  • The stacking ensemble (CatBoost + XGBoost + TabTransformer) outperforms all individual models on AUC-ROC (0.9717) and macro F1 (0.88)
  • TabTransformer, despite lower overall accuracy, captures high-risk women that tree-based models miss (Recall = 0.870), validating its role in the ensemble
  • Bayesian MCMC achieves excellent convergence (R-hat = 1.003, ESS = 3,477), enabling reliable uncertainty quantification for CHW decision support
  • Poverty (wealth index) and inadequate ANC are the dominant predictors, consistent with global maternal health evidence
  • Zero catastrophic misclassifications (High Risk predicted as Low Risk) on the test set — a critical safety property for triage tools
⚠️ Limitations & Ethical Considerations
  • Simulated data: Pilot uses 1,000 synthetic records; generalisability to real KDHS (n=10,391) requires validation
  • Self-reported data: KDHS responses subject to recall bias (e.g., ANC visit counts, delivery location)
  • Class imbalance: High Risk class (15%) — SMOTE mitigates but does not eliminate imbalance effects
  • Ethics: USIU-Africa IRB + NACOSTI approval pending; KDHS data use agreement signed; all data fully anonymised
  • CHW literacy: Interface validated against simulated users only; real CHW usability testing planned
  • Missing variables: v457 and v481 empty in KDHS 2022-IR release
